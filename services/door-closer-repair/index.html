Surface-mounted closers — the box you can see on the door or on the frame above it — are the bulk of what we get called to in Oshawa. Three failures cover nearly all of them: the body has lost hydraulic fluid, so the door either slams or drifts with no control at all; the arm, forearm or shoe bracket has torn loose from a door that was shouldered through or caught by a gust; or the unit was undersized for the opening in the first place and no amount of adjusting is going to hold it.

A closer is a sealed hydraulic unit. There is no fill port, so a leaking body cannot be topped up no matter what the internet says, and screwing the valves down on a dry unit is how the last of the fluid ends up on the floor. What we can do is tell you honestly whether the closer is worth adjusting or has to come off, size the replacement for the door width, weight and exposure it actually faces, and reinforce the mounting so the arm does not tear out of a chewed-up hollow-metal frame again next winter.

Most of these finish in a single visit. We carry common surface closers, arms, brackets and templates on the truck, so a slamming lobby door in a Vanier apartment building or a plaza entrance on Taunton Road usually gets sorted the same day rather than being wedged open until a part arrives.

FAQs

Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

My door closer is leaking oil — can it be refilled?

No. Modern closers are sealed hydraulic bodies with no fill port, so once the fluid is out the unit has to be replaced. A leaking closer either slams the door hard enough to split the frame or stalls short of the latch, and on a fire door that second failure is a code problem rather than an annoyance.

The door slams. Can you just turn a screw and fix it?

Sometimes, yes — sweep speed and latch speed are separate valves, and a door that races only at the very end is often a latch-speed adjustment. But if the body is dry or a valve has already been backed out too far, turning it further only makes things worse. We check the unit first and tell you which one you are actually dealing with.

How long should a commercial door closer last in Oshawa?

A properly sized unit on a normal commercial entrance lasts many years. What shortens it here is cycle count and weather — a plant personnel door on Thornton Road or a street entrance taking salt, grit and freeze-thaw wears far faster than an interior office door, and an undersized closer on a heavy exterior door fails early no matter what.
