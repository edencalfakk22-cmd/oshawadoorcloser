An exit device has one non-negotiable job: it releases the door in a single motion from the inside, with no key, no thumbturn and no special knowledge. Ontario’s Building Code calls for panic hardware on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred, which covers a great deal of Oshawa — the bars and restaurants around Simcoe and King, banquet halls, places of worship, school and campus gyms, arenas, theatres and the city’s recreation complexes.

The failures are predictable. Vertical-rod devices on double doors fall out of adjustment and the top latch stops engaging. Rim devices get dogged down permanently so the door never latches at all. Touch bars get bent by carts and pallet trucks, latches wear round and start slipping, and outside trim seizes after a winter of salt and grit. On fire-rated openings the device must not be dogged at all, and labelled fire exit hardware has to stay labelled — we do not substitute non-rated parts onto a rated assembly to save a trip.

We repair and adjust rim, mortise and surface vertical-rod devices, replace worn latches, dogging mechanisms and outside trim, sort out electric strikes and delayed-egress arrangements where they exist, and pair the device with a correctly set closer so the door latches cleanly instead of bouncing back off the strike.

FAQs

Exit Devices & Panic Hardware — your questions

Which Oshawa doors actually need panic hardware?

The Building Code requires it on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred — arenas, gyms, banquet halls, theatres, places of worship, bars and restaurants among them. Plenty of other commercial exits carry devices anyway, because they are the most reliable way to guarantee one-motion egress on a door people will meet in an emergency.

Our exit door will not open from the inside. How fast can you come?

That is a life-safety failure and we dispatch it as one. In the meantime do not chain it, do not block it and do not route people past it as though it works — station someone at the door and call us. Tell dispatch it is an egress failure so it goes to the front of the list.

Can we dog the bar down so the door stays unlatched during opening hours?

On a non-rated door, yes — mechanical dogging is a normal feature and we will set it up properly. On a fire-rated door, no: fire exit hardware must not be dogged, because the door has to latch for the separation to hold. If that door needs to stand open, a hold-open tied into the fire alarm is the answer.
