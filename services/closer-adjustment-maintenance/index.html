Closers are not a set-and-forget item in this climate. Between the cold thickening the hydraulic fluid, the summer humidity swelling doors, and the wind that comes across an exposed Durham parking lot in January, the same unit needs different settings in winter than it does in July. Most of the failures we get called out for in the middle of a cold snap were visible weeks earlier as a door that was closing a little slower every day.

A maintenance visit is straightforward. We walk the doors, check each closer for fluid loss and for arm or bracket damage, set sweep speed, latch speed and backcheck where the unit allows it, confirm every fire door still latches from any open position, and check opening force on barrier-free entrances so a door that was made heavier to beat a draft has not quietly become one nobody can pull open. The Building Code limits opening force on doors in a barrier-free path of travel, and cranking a spring up is the usual way that limit gets broken.

Buildings with a lot of openings — apartment blocks, schools, campus buildings, plazas, plants and office floors — get far more out of one scheduled pass than out of six separate emergency call-outs. We book the pre-winter round from early autumn and leave you a written list of what was adjusted and what needs replacing before the cold does it for us.

FAQs

Closer Adjustment & Preventive Maintenance — your questions

How often should commercial door closers be checked?

Twice a year suits most Oshawa buildings — once before the cold and once after it — with rated doors verified more often, because a self-closing device is required to be kept in operating condition rather than to have worked once on the day it was installed.

Can our own maintenance staff adjust the closers?

They can, and we will show them which valve does what while we are on site. What causes damage is guessing: sweep, latch and backcheck each control a different part of the swing, and backing one out too far dumps the remaining fluid and turns an adjustment into a replacement.

Why do all our doors get worse at the same time in winter?

Because the same three things hit them at once — colder fluid moving more slowly through the valves, doors and frames that have shifted with the temperature, and wind loading on the exposed entrances. That is exactly why an autumn round is worth booking: you correct a predictable seasonal shift instead of reacting to it in a snowstorm.
