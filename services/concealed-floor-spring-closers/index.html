When you cannot see a closer on an Oshawa entrance, it is either concealed inside the door or the transom above it, or it is a floor spring buried in the slab under the pivot. Both turn up constantly on the aluminum and all-glass entrances downtown, at the Oshawa Centre and the larger plazas, and across office, campus and institutional lobbies where nobody wanted a box hanging over the door.

Floor springs are the ones our winter hurts most. The cement case sits at grade, right in the path where meltwater, brine and grit get tracked in, and once the seal at the cover plate gives up the unit corrodes from the inside out. The symptoms are familiar: a door that no longer self-centres, a hold-open detent that has stopped working, oil weeping around the plate, or a leaf that has visibly dropped because the bottom pivot inside the spring has worn.

This work is more involved than a surface swap — a floor spring means lifting the door off, opening the case, and often re-shimming and re-aligning the whole leaf on its pivots. We do that, we replace concealed overhead closers in aluminum stiles and transoms, and where a floor-spring case is beyond saving we say so plainly and lay out the alternatives instead of burying labour in a unit that is going to fail again next winter.

FAQs

Concealed & Floor-Spring Closers — your questions

Why did our floor spring fail after only a few winters?

Salt and meltwater. A floor-spring case sits at grade directly in the traffic path, so brine and grit get carried straight onto it every time the door opens in February. Once the seal at the cover plate lets go, corrosion and contamination inside the case do the rest.

Can a concealed closer be replaced without a new door?

Usually yes. Overhead concealed units in an aluminum stile or transom can normally be swapped into the existing prep, and we template to match. If the prep was cut for a discontinued model we confirm exactly what fits before anything comes off the frame.

Our glass entrance door has dropped and drags. Is that the closer?

Often it is the pivot inside the floor spring rather than the closing mechanism itself. A worn bottom pivot lets the door sag, which is why it drags and stops latching. We check the bottom pivot, the top centre and the spring together instead of adjusting valves and hoping.
