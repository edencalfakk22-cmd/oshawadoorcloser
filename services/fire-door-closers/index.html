A fire door only does its job if it closes and latches without anyone touching it. Ontario’s Fire Code requires the closures in a fire separation to be maintained in operating condition, and the two findings we are called about most in Oshawa buildings are a door that swings shut but stops just short of latching, and a door that has been wedged, chained or propped open because someone found it inconvenient.

Propping is the first thing to stop. A wedged stairwell, corridor or garbage-room door defeats the separation it exists to create, and it is one of the easiest things for an inspector to write up. If traffic genuinely needs the door open, the correct answer is an electromagnetic hold-open tied into the fire alarm so the door releases and closes on an alarm signal — not a doorstop and not a fire extinguisher on the floor. The point matters more than usual in the rental houses around the Ontario Tech and Durham College campuses: those properties are licensed by the City, and Fire Code compliance is part of what the licence depends on, so a self-closing device that was quietly unhooked during a retrofit becomes a licensing problem as well as a safety one.

We work fire doors building-wide rather than one at a time: check each closer and self-closing device, confirm the door latches from any open position, correct latch speed and closer sizing, replace failed units, sort out the strike, latch and gasketing that are stopping the door seating properly, and hand back a written record of what was found and what was corrected for your fire safety plan file.

Fire Door Closers & Fire Code Compliance — your questions

Our fire door closes but does not latch. Is that a violation?

Treat it as one. A fire door has to close and latch on its own for the separation to hold, and a door resting against the strike is an open door as far as fire and smoke are concerned. It is a common finding in Oshawa buildings and usually corrects with latch-speed adjustment, proper closer sizing, or a strike and latch repair.

Can we prop the fire door open during move-in weekend or deliveries?

No. Wedging or chaining a fire door defeats the separation and is one of the most frequently written-up items in Durham multi-residential buildings. The compliant way to hold it open is a device released by the fire alarm, which we can install on most fire doors.

We are licensed under Oshawa's rental housing by-law — can you document the fire doors?

Yes. Licensed rental properties around the Durham College and Ontario Tech campuses have to meet the Fire Code along with the Building Code and property standards, so we test every self-closing device, correct what we can on the spot, list what needs parts, and give you a written record you can keep on file.
