Where we work in Oshawa

From Downtown Oshawa, O'Neill, McLaughlin, Vanier, Eastdale, Donevan, Lakeview, Farewell, Samac, Windfields, Taunton and Pinecrest, we cover every Oshawa neighbourhood — plus the surrounding Durham communities.

Fire doors in Oshawa apartment blocks and licensed student rentals. Ontario’s Fire Code requires the closures in a fire separation to be kept in operating condition, which in plain terms means the door has to swing shut and latch by itself, every time, with nobody helping it. The same findings come up over and over in Oshawa: a stairwell door propped with a fire extinguisher, a garbage-room door that closes but never latches, a suite door whose closer was unhooked years ago because a tenant complained it was heavy. In the neighbourhoods around the Ontario Tech and Durham College campuses the stakes are higher still — the City licenses residential rental housing there, and compliance with the Fire Code, Building Code and property standards is part of what that licence rests on. Self-closing devices that were disconnected during a bedroom retrofit are exactly what an inspection turns up. We work those buildings opening by opening and leave a written record of what was corrected.

Plant, warehouse and supplier buildings — Thornton Road and Northwood. Oshawa is still an assembly and logistics town, and industrial doors here take a beating no office door ever sees. The large distribution and manufacturing buildings along Thornton Road South, the supplier shops feeding the assembly plant, and the new employment blocks in Northwood Business Park near the airport and the 407 all run the same pattern: personnel doors in the fire separation between plant and office cycling hundreds of times a shift, doors clipped by pallet trucks and carts, and closer arms torn out of hollow-metal frames by a door that got shouldered through with both hands full. Cycle count is what kills a closer in a plant, not age. We size units for the traffic they actually see, reinforce the mounting rather than re-screwing into a chewed-out prep, and keep the rated openings latching so a fire watch never becomes the workaround.
